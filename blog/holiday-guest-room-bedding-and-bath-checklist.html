Quick answer

Start about two weeks before guests arrive. Check and protect the mattress, have one fitted sheet set on each bed plus a spare, add a warm layer guests can take on or off, and put out a bath towel, hand towel and washcloth for each guest. Finish with a bedside lamp, a free outlet, a few hangers and a note with the Wi-Fi details.

  • A clean, protected mattress and a choice of pillows
  • Sheets that fit the mattress depth, with a spare set
  • Layers: a light blanket, a comforter and one extra blanket
  • A full towel set per guest and a dry bath mat
  • A plan for overflow guests

A two-week prep timeline

Hosting is easier when the bedding is settled before the cooking starts, and two weeks leaves time for delivery and a first wash. This schedule works for Thanksgiving, the December holidays or New Year.

Two weeks before

Strip the guest bed and look at the mattress in daylight for stains, sagging and smells. Count your sheet sets and towels, and note the mattress size and depth. If more people are coming than you have beds, decide where everyone will sleep and order any extra sleeping surface now.

One week before

Wash everything that has been in the closet, even if it was clean when stored, because stored linens can pick up dust, a stale smell or pet hair. Wash new sheets and towels before first use, following the care label. Test the room's heat, the bedside lamp and the closet hangers.

Two to three days before

Make the bed, lay out the warm layers and stock the bathroom. If you have pets, do a final pass for hair and keep the door closed.

Arrival day

Air the room briefly if the weather allows, set a comfortable temperature, put out water and leave a welcome card on the nightstand.

Step 1: Get the bed right

The bed gets eight straight hours of use, so it deserves most of your attention. Work from the bottom up: mattress, protector, sheets, pillows, then layers.

Check and protect the mattress

Vacuum the surface, spot-clean marks according to the manufacturer's instructions and let it air out. Then add a mattress protector, which shields the mattress from spills and sweat and goes in the wash after each visit. The SafeRest waterproof mattress protector is one option on Amazon; check the size and depth against your mattress.

If the guest mattress is old or very firm, a topper is a cheaper fix than a new mattress. The Linenspa gel memory foam topper is listed in a queen size. A topper adds height, which matters when you choose fitted sheets.

Choose sheets that fit first

The classic guest-bed complaint is a fitted sheet that pops off a corner at night. Measure the mattress depth including any topper, then choose sheets with a clearly listed pocket depth that is comfortably larger. For thick beds, the Bedsure deep pocket sheet set is worth a look; confirm the stated depth on the product page.

  • Cotton percale feels crisp and cool, which suits warm sleepers and homes that run hot during holiday cooking. Bedsure lists a cotton percale weave sheet set.
  • Washed cotton is pre-washed for a softer, relaxed feel from the first night. See the Bedsure 6-piece washed cotton sheet set, and check what the six pieces are on the listing.
  • Cotton-linen blends have a textured look and breathe well in a small room with radiators. See the Bedsure cotton-linen blend sheet set.
  • Flannel is brushed cotton that feels warm straight away. It suits a cold or basement guest room but can be too warm in a well-heated house.

Offer a pillow choice

Pillows are the hardest thing for a guest to adjust to. Two per guest, one firmer and one softer, covers most sleepers. An adjustable pillow such as the Coop Home Goods Original Adjustable Pillow lets the sleeper add or remove fill, which helps when you do not know who will use the bed. No spare room? Extra sleeping space

Holiday visits often bring more people than beds. Plan the extra spots as carefully as the guest room.

  • Air mattress: flexible and easy to store. The King Koil Luxury Air Mattress is aimed at overnight guests; check size, height and pump details on the listing. Inflate it a few days early to check for leaks, and put a rug or blanket underneath on hard floors to block cold from below.
  • Sofa bed: open it before the visit, test the mechanism and add a topper if the mattress is thin. Measure it before buying sheets, as sizes vary.
  • Kids on the floor: a folded comforter under a sleeping bag turns a living room into a sleepover space.

Give every sleeper their own pillow, sheet and warm layer, and store the overflow kit together in one labelled bin.

Step 2: Stock the guest bathroom

Guests should be able to shower and get ready without asking where anything is.

Towels: how many and what kind

A workable rule is one bath towel, one hand towel and one washcloth per guest, with an extra bath towel per guest on longer stays. Bath sheets are larger and feel generous but take more hook and dryer space. Our best bath towels guide compares formats and set sizes, and the bath towels hub covers size, weight and care.

For a dedicated guest set, the Sleep & Beyond organic cotton 4 bath towel set covers a couple, and the 6-piece bath towel set stretches further. For volume on a budget, the Utopia Towels 8-piece set on Amazon includes bath, hand and wash towels. Check dimensions and colors before ordering.

Floor, hooks and small things

A dry bath mat keeps cold tile from being the first thing guests feel in the morning. The Gorilla Grip memory foam bath rug is a machine-washable option with grip underneath; check the size against your floor space. Add towel hooks, a wastebasket, a spare toilet roll in sight and a small basket of travel toiletries for anything a guest forgot.

Help towels dry

Damp towels start to smell quickly in a cool winter bathroom. Run the fan after showers, leave the door open when the room is free and give guests somewhere to hang towels flat. Our bedding and bath towel care guide covers washing and drying in more detail.

Step 3: Plan for cold nights

Guest rooms are often the coldest room in the house: at the end of a hallway, over a garage, or with vents closed for months. Sit in the room for an hour one evening before guests arrive to feel how it holds heat.

Layer instead of piling on

The most reliable setup is a sheet, a light blanket and a comforter or quilt, plus one extra blanket folded at the foot of the bed. A warm sleeper can kick off the comforter and keep the blanket; a cold sleeper can add the spare without searching the house. If you use a duvet, check the insert matches the cover and the bed, because an undersized insert leaves cold gaps at the sides.

Weighted blankets are a personal choice, so offer one as an option rather than the main layer, and never on a bed a young child will use.

Drafts, heat and dry air

Check windows for drafts, close heavy curtains at night and open closed vents a day or two early. If you use a space heater, follow the manufacturer's safety instructions, keep it well away from bedding and curtains, and do not leave it running unattended.

Forced-air heat can leave a room feeling dry. Some hosts add a small humidifier; if you do, clean and refill it according to the manufacturer's instructions. The US EPA's indoor air quality pages, linked below, offer general guidance.

Step 4: Light, noise and small comforts

Think about the moments guests struggle in an unfamiliar room: arriving tired after dark, getting up at night and getting ready in the morning.

  • Light: a lamp within reach of the bed, a night light in the hallway or bathroom, and curtains or blinds that actually close.
  • Power: a free outlet near the bed, ideally with a spare charging cable or small power strip on the nightstand.
  • Noise: holiday houses are loud, and guest rooms are often near the kitchen. A fan or white noise machine can mask household sounds, and it helps to mention what time the house usually wakes up.
  • Storage: a cleared drawer, a few empty hangers and a bench or chair so suitcases stay off the bed.

Finally, leave a short welcome card with the Wi-Fi details, where the extra blankets and towels are, how the heat works and anything quirky about the shower. It answers the questions guests are often too polite to ask.

Step 5: Pets, allergies and freshness

A room that looks clean can still bother a guest with allergies. Pet hair and dust collect on things that rarely get washed: the decorative throw, spare pillows, curtains and the upholstered chair.

  • Wash every textile in the room, not just the sheets.
  • Run a lint roller over upholstery, the headboard and the mattress before making the bed.
  • Keep pets out of the room for several days beforehand.
  • Vacuum the floor, under the bed and the curtains.
  • Ask guests about allergies and whether they need a feather-free pillow.

Pillow protectors keep guest pillows fresh between visits. Sleep & Beyond lists an organic cotton waterproof pillow case encasement pair that goes under the pillowcase; check the size and care instructions. With a mattress protector too, the whole bed resets with one load of laundry.

How many sets of sheets and towels do you need?

It depends on the length of stay and how quickly you can do laundry. Use this as a starting point.

Suggested linen counts per guest bed (rule of thumb, not a requirement)
Length of stay Sheet sets per bed Towels per guest Notes
One or two nights 1 on the bed, 1 spare 1 bath, 1 hand, 1 washcloth The spare covers spills
Three to five nights 1 on the bed, 1 spare 2 bath, 1 hand, 2 washcloths Swap towels midway
A week or longer 2 per bed, plus a spare 2 to 3 bath, 2 hand, 3 washcloths Change sheets mid-stay

For a less common bed size, such as a full or twin XL, buy the spare set at the same time as the first.

Common mistakes

  • Buying sheets without measuring. A topper or thick mattress can defeat a standard fitted sheet.
  • Skipping the first wash. New linens can feel stiff or smell of packaging.
  • One heavy comforter and nothing else. Guests end up too hot or too cold.
  • Forgetting overflow sleepers. The air mattress needs its own sheets, pillow and warm layer.
  • Testing nothing. Inflate the air mattress, switch on the lamp and run the shower before guests arrive.
  • Ordering too late. Confirm holiday delivery dates on the retailer's page before relying on an order.

Bottom line

A good holiday guest room does not need new furniture or a big budget. It needs a protected, clean mattress, sheets that fit, a pillow choice, layers guests can adjust and a bathroom stocked so nobody has to ask for a towel. Start two weeks out, measure before you buy, wash everything first and test anything that plugs in or inflates. If you only have time for three things, make them a fitted sheet that stays on, a spare blanket in plain sight and a full towel set for every guest.

Frequently asked questions

How far in advance should I prepare a guest room for the holidays?

About two weeks is comfortable. It leaves time to check the mattress, count linens, order anything missing and wash new sheets and towels. For Thanksgiving on November 26, 2026, take stock the week before and do the final setup two or three days ahead.

What bedding does a guest room need?

At minimum: a mattress protector, fitted and flat sheets, pillowcases, two pillows per guest, a light blanket and a comforter or quilt. Add a spare sheet set and a spare blanket to cover spills or a guest who sleeps cold.

How many towels should I put out for each guest?

One bath towel, one hand towel and one washcloth per guest is a practical start for a short stay. For three nights or more, plan a second bath towel per guest and keep a few spares in sight.

Which sheets are best for a guest bed in winter?

Flannel feels warm straight away and suits cold rooms. Cotton percale feels crisp and cool, which suits warm sleepers and well-heated homes, while washed cotton and cotton-linen blends sit in between. Whatever the fabric, make sure the pocket depth fits the mattress and any topper.

Is an air mattress good enough for holiday guests?

It can be if you treat it like a real bed. Inflate it a few days early to check for leaks, put a rug or blanket underneath on cold floors, and give it proper sheets, a pillow and a warm layer.

How do I prepare a guest room for someone with allergies?

Wash every textile in the room, vacuum the floor, curtains and under the bed, and keep pets out for several days beforehand. Use mattress and pillow protectors, and ask whether the guest needs a feather-free pillow.
